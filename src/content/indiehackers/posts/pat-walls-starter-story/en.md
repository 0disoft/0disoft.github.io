Starter Story, which Pat Walls started in 2017, was a site that asked founders how they started their businesses and recorded their revenue and customer acquisition processes. As interviews accumulated, it grew into a media business with a paid database, educational products, a newsletter, and a video channel. HubSpot's acquisition in February 2026 was the result of content accumulation combined with an audience, revenue models, and a production team.

## Looking for a Business That Could Be Run Alongside a Day Job

While working as a software developer, Walls spent about six months building enterprise SaaS with friends, but it failed. One of the problems he identified was time. To sell the product, they had to sell during the day while client companies were working, but because all the founders had day jobs, he looked for a new business that could make progress in the evenings and on weekends.

At first, he tried to build a content site around a topic that seemed likely to make money, but he couldn't find motivation in writing about an area he wasn't interested in. He then chose entrepreneurship, a subject he was curious about, and was influenced by Indie Hackers' founder-interview format. His differentiation was to cover many types of businesses, including consumer goods and e-commerce, rather than limiting himself to software.

The initial product was a small site with three interviews and an email signup link. He originally planned to publish after preparing 10 interviews, but because it was taking too long, he decided to launch first and gauge the response. He found founders to interview through acquaintances and handled content production and site development himself.

The first-month report written on December 1, 2017, records 1,200 visitor sessions, 27 newsletter subscribers, and five interviews. When a founder featured in an interview shared the post on their own Facebook, traffic increased significantly, but email subscribers mainly came from Reddit. At this point, Walls saw the difference between visitors who came to read an acquaintance's story and readers who wanted to keep receiving startup information.

## Turning Interview Production and Audience Acquisition Into Repeatable Work

At first, the process involved phone interviews lasting more than an hour, which were transcribed into articles of about 2,000 words. Later, he switched to having interviewees write their own answers and refined the Google Docs questionnaire to reduce follow-up questions and edits. Because it could take up to three weeks from sourcing to publication, standardizing the questions and collecting the answers was itself an important operational challenge.

The time he carved out to work alongside his day job was two hours a day. At first, he worked at Starbucks after getting off work, but when fatigue made it hard to concentrate, he switched to waking up at 6 a.m. and working before leaving for work. According to his retrospective, by October 2018 Starter Story had reached USD 3,000 in monthly revenue, and he began planning to leave his company.

Even after he was able to work full time, the question of which business to focus on remained. Pat Walls later revealed in a retrospective that in May 2020 he had been juggling two businesses and several side projects while working about 80 hours a week. He wound down the other projects and decided to focus on Starter Story, which he judged to have the greatest potential.

Around the same time, he also changed the way he brought in readers. As sharing interviews on Reddit lost effectiveness, he reframed existing case studies around search demand, with topics such as 'business ideas for people who love golf.' Walls explained that this search engine optimization strategy increased organic search traffic roughly tenfold over the next year. Re-editing the accumulated interviews to match the questions readers were searching for was the turning point.

## Monetizing the Archive and Building a Team

The revenue model consisted of paid membership, advertising and sponsorship, and affiliate fees. The membership, introduced around 2019, charged an annual fee and provided unlimited access to case studies and a database of business ideas. Advertisers paid to promote their products to the site's and newsletter's readers interested in entrepreneurship, while affiliate fees were generated when readers signed up for or purchased featured tools.

The revenue Walls disclosed for September 2022 was USD 74,000. In October of that year, he explained that revenue composition was about 38% membership, about 57% advertising and sponsorship, and about 5% affiliate fees, with roughly 2,500 paying members. Then, in August 2023, he said Starter Story had reached USD 1 million in annual revenue.

A major catalyst for expanding the team came in 2020. Shortly after the pandemic began, he asked his sister, who had lost her job, to help with a few articles; a few months later, she joined full time. Walls credited her with contributing significantly to the business's growth by pushing forward projects he had not been able to focus on enough.

Operations combined in-house development with outside talent. The company's published examples of tool use include using freelancers through Upwork, getting feedback on video production through Frame.io, and connecting work tools through Zapier. It built systems not only for creating content directly but also for managing production and collaboration.

## Expanding from a text-focused site into a video and education business

The channel that gained greater weight in the later growth phase was YouTube. In the February 2026 acquisition coverage, Pat Walls explained that long-form founder interview videos were recording 1.5 million to 2 million views per month. It was an extension into video of the format he had built in writing: asking real business owners how they specifically operate.

The products for sale also broadened from access to case studies into courses and a paid community. According to the same report, about 75% of revenue immediately before the acquisition came from its own products, including subscriptions, courses, and the paid community. From a structure in 2022 in which advertising and sponsorship accounted for more than half, the weight shifted toward products that readers paid for directly.

The scale HubSpot announced at the time of the acquisition was more than 800,000 combined YouTube channel subscribers and about 300,000 newsletter subscribers. Its database had accumulated more than 4,500 founder case studies and interviews, and annual content views were presented as more than 100 million. At this point, Starter Story was a media brand securing readers across multiple channels.

## What HubSpot Acquired Was Content and Reader Relationships

On February 23, 2026, Adweek reported HubSpot Media's acquisition of Starter Story. At the time, there were three full-time staff members: Walls, his sister and COO Sam, and producer Gus Tiffer, all of whom joined HubSpot Media. The acquisition price and financial terms were not disclosed.

The reason HubSpot officially gave for the acquisition had to do with the nature of the audience. Starter Story's readers were early-stage founders deciding what software and tools to use as they started businesses, which matched the customer group HubSpot wanted to reach. HubSpot presented a strategy of owning media that readers already trusted, and it placed Starter Story in the same trajectory as its earlier acquisition, The Hustle.

As of October 2026, the Starter Story homepage states that signing up for the newsletter gives free access to business data. The site includes a link to get started with HubSpot for free, and the sign-up process also includes a notice about being contacted regarding HubSpot's products and services. This reflects how the acquisition strategy of connecting founders who read the content with software customers is visible in the actual service interface.

What developed into a business asset in this case was the combination of the original interviews, the readers they attracted, the products readers bought, and the team that continued to produce them. Walls added distribution, sales, and a production system one after another to the documentation work he had started alone, and that structure created a business HubSpot could acquire.
